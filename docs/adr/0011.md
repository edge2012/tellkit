# 0011 — Code-generated frames

**Context.** The instructional image must preserve object identity, counts and spatial relationships across time. A visually attractive frame is insufficient when it changes the relation being taught. The route comparison put image-model keyframes at about 20 times the cost and direct generated video at about 140 times the cost of frame drawing.

**Alternatives.** One route uses image models for keyframes or asks a model to generate video directly. The other renders every frame from explicit geometry and action state. The model route was rejected because its higher estimated cost came with uncontrolled relationship correctness, making visual polish an unreliable substitute for instructional accuracy.

**Decision.** Choose code-generated frames and encode their sequence. Objects, relations and action timing remain inspectable inputs to drawing. Single-episode rendering improved from 555 seconds to 46 seconds on the same content batch, and individual frame drawing took 2.3–3.8 milliseconds. These timings give a concrete performance reason to continue investing in the frame renderer.

**Consequences.** Every supported shape and action requires implementation, and the visual vocabulary remains bounded by that work. Geometry rules can still be wrong, so deterministic output needs validation rather than trust by default. The gain is control over relationships and repeatability; it does not remove the cost of designing readable instructional scenes.
